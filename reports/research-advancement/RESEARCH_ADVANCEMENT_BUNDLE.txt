## 1. Mission and scientific objective

Execute an end-to-end research cycle that repairs scientific measurement, identifies the actual
performance bottleneck, implements one evidence-driven improvement, and produces research evidence
and understandable professor-facing reports throughout the work.

Preserve the original project: a world model predicts what diagnostic evidence could reveal, and a
selector uses those imagined outcomes to choose real acquisitions. Study both when this helps and
when a flawed model makes decisions worse. Do not replace this with a larger-backbone-only project,
an unrelated classification benchmark, or a new orchestration platform.

Central question:
“When does improving a world model improve diagnostic decisions, and which prediction errors,
evidence-update errors, or acquisition assumptions make imagination ineffective or harmful?”

Separate three outcomes:
- Engineering validity: the code computes the declared quantities and enforces the protocol.
- Empirical benefit: the intervention improves a predeclared accuracy–cost objective against strong controls.
- Publication evidence: the contribution is differentiated, explained, and supported beyond its development setting.

Passing tests establishes neither the second nor the third. A negative or inconclusive experiment
can complete a milestone when it answers its question honestly. No accuracy gain or ICML acceptance
is guaranteed or required to justify reporting completed work.

Execute, rather than stopping at recommendations. When permission or access blocks an experiment,
complete independent authorized work, prepare the exact request, and deliver a useful partial packet.
Do not bypass a scientific gate, repeatedly broaden the search, or wait indefinitely for a positive result.

## 6. M1 — Repair clinical and synthetic integrity before selecting a method

### 6.1 Matched predictive scoring and reference distributions

Check REVIEW-001 at the current content ref. For the declared per-item metric, learned and reference
models must score identical item targets with identical inclusion, conditioning, smoothing conventions,
and aggregation weights:

NLL = -(1/N) × sum over evaluated items of log q(observed outcome | permitted history, panel).

Use the mixture's per-item predictive probabilities for learned item NLL. Fit the declared reference
separately from pooled TRAIN outcomes under the matching chart-availability condition. A population
class-prior mixture is not automatically the pooled charted-data marginal.

Keep joint-panel likelihood only as a separately named metric with a comparable reference. Do not
silently change missing-item inclusion or target weighting while describing a metric repair. Skill is
undefined when its reference denominator is undefined or zero; reject nonfinite gate statistics.

Required independent fixtures: hand-computed single- and multi-item cases; identical predictive
distributions giving equal losses and zero skill where defined; diagnosis-dependent chart availability;
rare/unseen/absent outcomes; and explicit NLL/Brier/ECE denominators. Do not merely compare production
code with another helper implementing the same mistake. Single-item fixtures cannot catch the
joint-versus-item defect. Do not predict whether corrected clinical skill will remain positive.

### 6.2 Synthetic split, cost, and source-provenance repairs

Bring REVIEW-002, REVIEW-006, and REVIEW-011 into M1, not M5.

Namespace environment and behavior-policy RNG streams by purpose/split. Verify underlying generated
cases, not just IDs containing different split names. Preserve identical evaluation realizations
across arms while separating training, development, and test streams. Use synthetic case fingerprints;
private clinical identifiers/fingerprints are not automatically exportable.

Adjudicate old likelihood evaluation separately from old utility evaluation: the identified same-seed
likelihood sample repeats training cases, while the default utility evaluation used an offset seed.
A finite-sample learned-model score exceeding an oracle is not itself proof of leakage.

For the existing synthetic one-step information objective with diagnosis-independent missingness and
free unavailable results, expected monetary cost is p_available × cost. The matching score is:

score(a) = p_available(a) × [IG(a) − lambda × provided_cost(a)].

Account for consumed actions/horizon separately. Do not silently introduce a request penalty or
claim that a free request consumes no step. For future informative-missingness environments, include
the unavailable observation's information where the model assumes it; the simple expression above
must not be generalized without checking those assumptions.

Test p=0, p=1, intermediate availability, action ranking, and stopping against enumerated outcomes.
Preserve legacy outputs, regenerate affected evidence in a new namespace, and record a clean source
revision or an explicit immutable source snapshot/patch digest. A base SHA that omits executable
working-tree changes is insufficient provenance.

Register the matched-information comparator needed for REVIEW-007 before any general planning-
superiority claim. Its performance study can follow in M3; it need not block deterministic M1 checks.

## 7. M2 — Measure headroom, diagnose mechanisms, and supply a reader probe

### 7.1 Actual intervention opportunities and errors

Privately audit authorized validation evidence for nonanswers, invalid/repeated requests, class-specific
errors, correct-to-incorrect regressions, and intervention opportunities. Define an opportunity using
the actual pre-divergence baseline path, guard precedence, turn/token limits, valid candidates, and a
permitted nontrivial replacement—not simply “a lab request occurred.”

For the unchanged lab-only intervention, calculate the optimistic recall-gain bound:

G_max = (100/4) × sum over classes k of E_k_eligible / N_k.

E_k_eligible counts baseline errors in class k where this intervention can act; N_k is that class's
cohort size. The bound assumes every eligible error is corrected and no correct case regresses.
State deterministic/shared-path assumptions and report eligible baseline-correct cases as regression
exposure. Do not apply this bound after changing the reader, stopping, or intervention scope.

Assign error-mechanism categories only when the evidence supports them. A difficult error is not
necessarily an ambiguous case. Small intervention headroom limits the current treatment, not every
possible future method.

### 7.2 Evidence identity, support, and missingness

Audit actual panel mappings and observation provenance before asserting empirical double counting.
Export aggregate overlap statistics only. Where panels expose the same underlying measurement,
introduce a canonical unique-evidence representation using identity, units, and occurrence/time when
available. Preserve genuinely distinct repeats. State uncertainty when the dataset lacks identity.

Test idempotent re-exposure and preservation of distinct observations. An order-invariant updater
must be order invariant; a temporal model must instead document its legitimate time dependence.
Keep legacy full-target scoring separate from a prospective new-information metric that excludes
already revealed measurements.

Check whether item-independence sampling assigns mass to impossible “given available” combinations,
including all-absent panels, and inconsistent shared items. Quantify the mass in enumerable fixtures
and authorized aggregates before claiming empirical harm. Distinguish unseen, unreported-within-panel,
unavailable-panel, and observed values.

Missingness may encode historical workup. Do not assume it is intrinsically uninformative, equivalent
to normal physiology, or automatically usable as a transportable clinical signal. A lexical HPI screen
is limited evidence, not proof of no copying/leakage. Inspect observation access and timing/provenance
within the protected workflow. Distributional tertiles are not clinical normal/abnormal flags.

### 7.3 Gain semantics and truth-referenced usefulness

Check predictive/update consistency after absent-factor exclusion or likelihood tempering. In a tiny
enumerable model, compare the implemented gain with exact calculations and quantify Monte Carlo error.
For coherent Bayesian prediction, expected posterior belief equals prior belief and expected entropy
reduction has the mutual-information interpretation. An arbitrary normalized tempered update does not
establish that identity.

Either justify a coherent pair or explicitly retain an expected entropy-change surrogate. Do not
silently clip negatives. A realized entropy increase may be legitimate; estimated negatives can also
reflect Monte Carlo error or inconsistent updates. The historical implementation already advances a
single RNG across draws: do not “repair” a repeated-seeding bug absent from current code.

Alongside entropy diagnostics, measure correct-label log-probability change, Brier change, diagnostic
accuracy change, selected-action utility, and synthetic decision regret. Labels are used only privately
for permitted training/evaluation, never ordinary action selection. Correlation of ten gain-bin means
with the same updater's entropy changes is not probability calibration or proven diagnostic benefit.

Evaluate new-information prediction and action rankings on policy-relevant histories where possible.
Eight permutations of one admission do not create eight independent people.

### 7.4 Full tuning-path audit

Track prior temperature, likelihood temperature, model/representation selection, thresholds, and any
use of label-informed development results. C1's tau cross-validation does not isolate the entire tuned
pipeline. Keep the historical 0.637 result labeled developmental and partially out of fold.

For a successor generalization estimate, isolate all applicable tuning in inner training partitions
or a full outer-fold design. Keep every prefix/order/derived observation from an admission together;
use subjects when available. Document exposure of fixed components as well. Choosing a candidate on
out-of-fold scores still requires later independent evaluation of that selection process.

### 7.5 Bounded reader probe — prerequisite for M3

Implement at most one initial CPU reader-probe candidate after the representation/metric repairs.
This is explicitly allowed before M3 and is not the integrated M4 method. A calibrated, regularized
partial-evidence classifier is the default probe unless the audit supports a better modest choice.
Use train-only fitting/tuning with the exposure labels above; do not call the candidate “improved”
until a comparison establishes the relevant improvement.

Replay existing authorized acquired evidence with a fixed endpoint and stopping convention. Match
permissible information. If one reader omits imaging/exam content or uses coarse bins while another
uses richer text, label the comparison representation-plus-reader, not a pure reader ablation.
Provide a matched-information comparison where feasible, or state the narrower estimand.

An all-available-evidence probe is separate, privileged, and not a guaranteed upper bound or deployable
method. Structured replay may be CPU-only; LLM replay may require GPU approval. Never assume all-evidence
inference is free or that unavailable outcomes exist.

Acceptance: quantified headroom, overlap/support assessment, gain/tuning diagnostics, ranked bottleneck
hypotheses, and a runnable bounded reader probe for M3—or an explicit blocker and the independent
synthetic/CPU results. The integrated architecture is not selected merely because one probe looks good.

## 8. M3 — Isolate prediction, acquisition, updating, and final interpretation

### 8.1 Mandatory arm-component contract

Every arm declares all of the following before running:

| Component | Required declaration |
|---|---|
| Outcome predictor | Model identity, conditioning, outcome support, sampling budget |
| Belief updater | Update semantics, evidence identity, calibration/tempering |
| Final diagnosis reader | Input information, representation, decision rule |
| Availability information | Learned, population, observed, or privileged; update behavior |
| Acquisition | Trigger, eligible actions, replacement and tie rules |
| Stopping/guards | Unchanged or explicitly intervened upon |
| Budgets | Provided-test cost, request/action horizon, tokens, computational allowance |
| Information access | All ordinary and privileged inputs |
| Pairing/provenance | Cases, seeds by purpose, source and fitted-artifact identities |

A comparison advertised as changing only selection holds all other relevant components fixed. If
changing the updater also changes the reader or stopping behavior, name that joint treatment.
Matching function names across adapters is not enough; observation, cost, and termination semantics
must match or be explicitly distinguished.

### 8.2 Efficient experimental sequence

Use the M2 alternative reader, without assuming it is better:

| Acquisition | Existing reader | Alternative reader |
|---|---|---|
| Strong simple selection | Reference | Interpretation/representation probe |
| Learned content selection | Selection probe | Integrated interaction probe |
| Precisely defined privileged selection | Privileged control | Privileged joint control |

Proceed in three steps:
1. Fixed-evidence replay: compare interpretation at identical evidence and endpoints.
2. Fixed-component acquisition: compare selectors with updater, reader, availability, eligibility,
   stopping, and budgets held fixed for the intended contrast.
3. Closed-loop integration: evaluate feedback and reader-by-selector interactions separately.

Report paired contrasts and the interaction when the design identifies it. Do not infer clean main
effects from a table whose other components also change. Do not execute every cell by default if a
smaller staged design answers the question; freeze which cells and contrasts are required first.

Include V1 fallback and V3 guards as prospectively declared whole-system references. Preserve their
historical primary comparisons. Do not present V1 and V3 as identical acquisition mechanisms.

Retain the existing synthetic model-free comparator with an accurate name: coarse, model-dependent,
one-step value baseline. Before a broad planning-superiority claim, add one matched-information
non-planning value/policy comparator using the same allowed belief/history and acquisition mask,
comparable training episodes, and an explicitly justified observed task-return target. A learned
belief-based reward is not an independent terminal-return baseline.

### 8.3 Oracle and population-control interpretation

Keep the historical clinical oracle identified as a true-outcome AND true-availability, fixed-updater
control. It is not an optimal diagnostician or a guaranteed task-performance ceiling.

A content-only control holds availability information fixed where outcome support allows identification.
In retrospective clinical charts, unavailable outcomes remain unknown. Restricting all arms to known
charted support gives all of them additional support information: label that restricted privileged
subexperiment, not an ordinary deployable comparison. Never fill unknown outcomes with model samples
and score them as observed truth. Use synthetic data for clean fully counterfactual separation.

Distinguish a ground-truth generative model from advance knowledge of realized outcomes. A genuine
small-environment optimum must obey the same observation access and budget as its comparator; exact
search/dynamic programming under known dynamics does not grant clairvoyance.

Separate population-predictor-only controls from population-belief-policy controls. Preserve the
patient-specific evaluator belief when only the predictor is being ablated. Separate synthetic oracle
selection from oracle updating; the historical joint oracle cannot identify either effect alone.

### 9.2 Declare the planning quantity and target reader

Every selector must identify its objective: coherent mutual information, expected entropy-change
surrogate, one-step task-risk reduction, or a specified finite-horizon return.

A useful initial decision-value prototype is:

Q_hat(a | h) = R_hat(h)
              − E_[o ~ p_phi(. | h,a)] R_hat(h plus a,o)
              − lambda × E[actual charged acquisition cost | h,a].

Freeze before evaluation: loss, class weighting, final reader/decision rule, risk-estimation procedure,
outcome support, unavailable-result behavior, monetary normalization, remaining horizon, and stopping.
R_hat targets the error/loss of the reader actually used, not an unrelated model's confidence.

For example, a calibrated label distribution can estimate the loss of that reader's committed action,
or an error model can estimate conditional reader loss. Both require validation; normalization alone
does not make either estimate correct. Class weights must be chosen from permitted training/design
information, not hidden test labels used at action time. Report any difference between the optimized
utility and historical macro-recall T5.

Include UNAVAILABLE in the expected transition where applicable. Charge provided-test dollars only
when the environment does so; represent consumed steps separately. One-step risk reduction is not
silently treated as optimal multistep value. Under a lab-only rerank, compare candidate advantage with
the proposed baseline lab action; do not introduce an unauthorized stop action.

Compare against the existing information-gain rule and the strong controls needed to distinguish the
hypothesis. Changing readers, stopping, imaging scope, or cost semantics is a named intervention with
its own ablation, not a hidden implementation detail.
